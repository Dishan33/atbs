Block 1 — Personality: Concept · Determinants · Type & Trait Theories

Personality: Concept & Definition

Allport (1937): "Dynamic organisation within the person of psychophysical systems that determine unique adjustments to environment." Two pillars: Individuality + Consistency. Character = moral judgement. Temperament = inborn characteristics. Both parts of personality but NOT synonymous.

Determinants of Personality

Formula: Personality = Biological × Psychological × Environmental (multiplicative). Watson = extreme environmentalist.
5 Biological: Body build (somatotypes), Attractiveness (Brislin & Lewis, 1968), Homeostasis, Physical defects (Adler's organ inferiority), Health.
5 Psychological: Intellectual, Emotional balance ("mental health insurance"), Catharsis, Self-disclosure, Aspiration/Achievement (delusion of grandeur, superiority complex).
6 Environmental: Social acceptance (isolation → sour-grapes), Social deprivation, Educational, Family (identification/imitation), Ordinal position (firstborn/lastborn/middle), Family size.

Type Theories

Hippocrates/Galen (~5th BC)

Blood→Sanguine (cheerful). Phlegm→Phlegmatic (apathetic). Black bile→Melancholic (sad). Yellow bile→Choleric (irritable). Baseless today.

Sheldon (1942) — Somatotypes

Ectomorph (thin→artistic, introverted; cerebrotonic). Endomorph (round→relaxed, sociable; viscerotonic). Mesomorph (muscular→dominant, energetic; somatotonic). 1–7 scale. Weak methodology (Tylor, 1965).

Jung (1922) & MBTI

4 dimensions: E/I, S/N, T/F, J/P (J-P added by Isabel Myers). 16 personality types. Jung's task = Individuation. Keirsey temperaments: Guardian, Rational, Idealist, Artisan.

Type A & Type B (Friedman)

Type A = workaholic, driven (coronary risk). Type B = laid back.

Limitations of Types

Discontinuous, all-or-nothing. "Types exist in the eye of the beholder whereas traits exist within the people."

Trait Theories

Allport (1937, 1961)

Lexical approach (17,953 words, Allport & Odbert, 1936). Idiographic.
Cardinal (pervasive; e.g., Gandhi→non-violence), Central (~5–10; building blocks), Secondary (situation-specific).
Proprium = self. Seven propriate functions: (1) Bodily self (2) Self-identity (3) Self-esteem (4) Self-extension (5) Self-image (6) Rational coper (7) Propriate striving.
Functional autonomy: Adult motives independent of original origins. "Means becomes end in itself." Preservative vs Propriate.
Mature personality — 6 attributes: Extended self, Warm interactions (intimacy + compassion), Emotional security, Realistic perception, Self-insight & humour, Unifying philosophy of life.
Study of Values (Allport, 1960; based on Spranger): Theoretical, Economic, Aesthetic, Social, Political, Religious.

Cattell (1950)

Surface vs Source traits (16 source → 16 PF). Source traits = better predictors.
Ergs (10 innate: hunger, sex, gregariousness, etc.) + Sentiments (environmental).
MAVA: 1/3 heredity, 2/3 environment. Specification Equation: R = f(S, P).
Three data sources: L-data, Q-data, OT-data. Syntality = group traits.
Global traits: Introversion-Extraversion, Openness vs Tough-Mindedness, Self-Controlled vs Unrestrained.

Eysenck (1916–1997) — PEN Model

4-level hierarchy: Specific responses → Habits → Traits → Superfactors.
Three superfactors (orthogonal): E (Extraversion), N (Neuroticism), P (Psychoticism).
E via ARAS (introverts higher arousal; Yerkes-Dodson Law). N via limbic system. P via testosterone/MAO.
EPQ + Lie Scale. Lemon Drop Test (Corcoran, 1964): introverts secrete 2× saliva.
High I + High N → anxiety disorders. High E + High N → psychopathic disorders.

Big Five / FFM (OCEAN)

Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism.
Goldberg (1981, lexical) vs McCrae & Costa (1987, questionnaire). NEO-PI-R: 240 items, 6 facets.
Mischel (1968): person-situation debate. Stable 3–30 years.
Theoretical perspectives: Interpersonal (Sullivan), Socioanalytic (Hogan=reputation), Evolutionary (Buss), FFT (basic tendencies vs characteristic adaptations).

Guilford (1959, 1988)

7 modalities. GZTS (10 bipolar traits). Structure-of-Intellect: 120→180 factors. Three levels: hexic, primary, type.

Block 2 — Theories of Personality I (Psychodynamic · Social Cognitive · Learning · Humanistic)

Freud — Psychoanalytic Theory

Structure

Topographical: Conscious (~10%), Preconscious (~10–15%), Unconscious (~75–80%). Iceberg.
Structural: Id (pleasure principle, unconscious), Ego (reality principle, mediates), Superego (ego-ideal + conscience, all levels).
Instincts: Eros (life/love) + Thanatos (death). Libido = psychic energy.

Defense Mechanisms

Denial, Repression, Regression, Reaction formation, Projection, Rationalisation, Intellectualisation, Displacement, Sublimation (most adaptive).

Psychosexual Stages

StageAgeZone/Key
Oral0–18moMouth; fixation→smoking, dependent
Anal18mo–3yrAnus; retentive→perfectionist, expulsive→messy
Phallic3–6yrGenitals; Oedipus/Electra, castration anxiety, identification
Latency6yr–pubertyDormant libido; same-sex play
Genitalpuberty+Sexual urges reawaken; opposite-sex peers

Evaluation

Merits: complete theory, unconscious, defense mechanisms. Demerits: lacking empirical evidence, over-emphasis on sex, psychic determinism.

Karen Horney (1885–1952)

Basic anxiety = isolated/helpless in hostile world. Basic evil → basic hostility.
10 neurotic needs. 3 coping strategies: Compliance (moving-toward), Aggression (moving-against), Withdrawal (moving-away-from).
Theory of Self: Real self vs Ideal self → "tyranny of the should," "search for glory." Womb envy. Opposed penis envy. Neo-Freudian.

Sullivan (died 1949) — Interpersonal Theory

Interpersonal model (vs Freud's drive model). 3 levels of cognition: Prototaxic, Parataxic, Syntaxic.
Self-system = most inclusive dynamism. Security operations: dissociation, selective inattention.
3 personifications: Good-me, Bad-me, Not-me.
7 developmental epochs: Infancy→Childhood→Juvenile→Pre-adolescence→Early adolescence→Late adolescence→Adulthood. Timing dictated by social environment.
Euphoria = no anxiety. Anxiety = chief disruptive force. "Mothering one."

Bandura — Social Cognitive Theory

Triadic reciprocal determinism: P↔B↔E. "People are both products and producers of their environment."
Self-efficacy = belief in one's capabilities. 4 sources: (1) Mastery experiences (most effective), (2) Social modeling, (3) Social persuasion, (4) Psychological responses.
Bobo Doll Experiment: children imitated aggression without reward → contradicted behaviourism.
4 steps of modeling: Attentional → Retentional → Reproduction → Motivational.
Vicarious learning. Symbolic modeling.
Limitations: behaviour more consistent than theory suggests; biological factors ignored; theory not unified.

Pavlov — Classical Conditioning

UCS→UCR; CS paired with UCS→CR. Acquisition (pairings + interval ~0.5s). Generalisation, Discrimination, Extinction, Spontaneous recovery. Higher-order conditioning.
Experimental neurosis (Shenger-Krestovnika, 1921): circle vs ellipse discrimination breakdown.
Applications: Systematic desensitisation, flooding, aversion therapy, counter-conditioning.

Skinner — Operant Conditioning

Operant (voluntary) vs Respondent (involuntary). Reinforcer (positive/negative). Aversive = punishment.
Schedules: Continuous, Fixed ratio, Fixed interval, Variable ratio, Variable interval (most resistant to extinction).
Shaping = successive approximation. Superstitious behaviour (no causal relationship). Token economy.
Criticisms: Chomsky (1959) — language not explained. Boulding (1984) — animal principles may not apply. Not comprehensive personality theory. Biological factors ignored.

Maslow — Hierarchy of Needs

"Third Force" (vs psychodynamic + behaviourist). Holistic. Focus on potentials.
5 levels: Physiological → Safety → Belongingness → Esteem (lower=respect of others, higher=self-respect) → Self-actualisation.
D-needs (deficiency; first 4; homeostasis). B-needs (being/growth; self-actualisation; continuous).
Self-actualisers: reality-centered, problem-centered, autonomous, unhostile humour, peak experiences, democratic value, creativity, resist enculturation. Imperfections: realistic anxiety, ruthlessness, metapathologies.
Criticism: methodology (biographical analysis); ~2% achieve; constraints on self-actualisation (Goldstein & Rogers disagree).

Rogers — Person-Centered / Self Theory

Self = organised perceptions of who I am. Real self vs Ideal self. Congruence = healthy; incongruence = anxiety.
Unconditional positive regard (vs conditional). Fully functioning person: open to experience, existential, trusts organism, creative, harmonious.
3 therapeutic conditions: Genuineness, Unconditional positive regard, Empathic understanding.
Key books: Client-Centered Therapy (1951), On Becoming a Person (1961).

Block 3 — Theories of Personality II (Allport · Cattell · Eysenck · Big Five)

Allport — Dispositional Theory

Definition (1937): "Dynamic organisation within the individual of psychophysical systems that determine unique adjustment to environment."
8 characteristics of traits (1966, "Traits Revisited"): real, generalised beyond habit, dynamic, empirically established, relatively independent, not moral judgement, idiographic + nomothetic, inconsistent acts don't disprove.
3 types: Cardinal (pervasive; Gandhi), Central (~5–10; building blocks), Secondary (situation-specific).
Common vs Individual traits. Idiographic — individual more important.
Proprium = self. 7 propriate functions: Bodily self, Self-identity, Self-esteem, Self-extension, Self-image, Rational coper, Propriate striving.
Two modes: Opportunistic (reactive, past-oriented, biological) vs Propriate (proactive, future-oriented, psychological).
Functional autonomy: Adult motives independent of origins. "Means becomes end." Preservative (repetitious) vs Propriate (master motivation system).
6 attributes of mature personality: Extended self, Warm interactions (intimacy + compassion), Emotional security, Realistic perception, Self-insight & humour, Unifying philosophy of life.

Cattell — Trait Theory

Inductive method + factor analysis. Personality = pattern that predicts behaviour.
Surface vs Source traits (16 source → 16 PF). Source = better predictors; underlying structure.
16 PF source traits: Warmth, Reasoning, Emotional Stability, Dominance, Liveliness, Rule-Consciousness, Social Boldness, Sensitivity, Vigilance, Abstractness, Privateness, Apprehension, Openness to Change, Self-Reliance, Perfectionism, Tension.
Global traits: (A) Introversion-Extraversion, (B) Openness vs Tough-Mindedness, (C) Self-Controlled vs Unrestrained.
Ergs (10 innate: hunger, sex, gregariousness, etc.) + Sentiments (environmental). SEMs (socially shaped).
MAVA: 1/3 heredity, 2/3 environment. Specification Equation: R = f(S, P).
3 data sources: L-data, Q-data, OT-data. Syntality = group traits.

Eysenck — Trait-Type Theory (PEN Model)

4-level hierarchy: Specific responses → Habits → Traits → Superfactors.
3 superfactors (orthogonal, normally distributed):
E (Extraversion): sociable, impulsive vs reserved, introspective.
N (Neuroticism): anxious, moody vs calm, stable.
P (Psychoticism): troublesome, hostile vs empathic, conventional.
Biological basis:
• E via ARAS (Ascending Reticular Activating System). Introverts = higher cortical arousal → more sensitive. Yerkes-Dodson Law (inverted-U).
• N via limbic system (hippocampus, amygdala, septum, hypothalamus). Neurotics = lower thresholds.
• P via testosterone + MAO (monoamine oxidase). Low MAO = vulnerability marker.
EPQ (Eysenck Personality Questionnaire) + Lie Scale. JEPQ for children 7–15.
Lemon Drop Test (Corcoran, 1964): introverts secrete 2× saliva.
Psychopathology link: High I + High N → anxiety disorders. High E + High N → psychopathic disorders.

Type Theories (from Block 3, Unit 3)

Ayurvedic doshas: Vata (ectomorph), Pitta (mesomorph), Kapha (endomorph).
Jung/MBTI: E/I, N/S, T/F, J/P → 16 types. Individuation.
Block (1971): Well-adjusted/resilient, Overcontrolling, Undercontrolled.

Big Five / Five-Factor Model

OCEAN: Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism.
Big Five = taxonomy (Goldberg, lexical, phenotypic, circular, adjectives). FFM = theoretical (McCrae & Costa, biosocial, hierarchical, sentences).
5th factor: "Intellect/Imagination" (lexical) vs "Openness to Experience" (questionnaire).
Measurement: NEO-PI-R (240 items, 6 facets, Costa & McCrae 1992). BFI (44 items, John). IPIP (Goldberg, public domain). BFAS (DeYoung, 100 items, 2 aspects each).
FFT (McCrae & Costa, 1996): Basic tendencies (stable, genetic) vs Characteristic adaptations (changeable, environmental).
Theoretical perspectives: Interpersonal (Sullivan/Wiggins), Socioanalytic (Hogan=reputation), Evolutionary (Buss=difference detecting), Social Investment (Roberts).
Characteristics: Dimensions (not types); stable 45 years; heritable. "Psychology of the stranger."
Criticisms: Eysenck insists only 3 (PEN). Block (1995) — factor analysis inadequate. Traits beyond Big Five (impulsive, sensation-seeking).

Technical Criteria

4 criteria: (1) Standardisation — uniform procedures. (2) Norms — raw→percentile; representative group. (3) Reliability — consistency (test-retest >+.70; split-half; parallel forms; inter-scorer). (4) Validity — measures what intended (content; criterion-related: predictive, concurrent, construct; convergent vs divergent [Campbell & Fiske, 1959]; Cronbach & Meehl, 1955).

Self-Report Inventories

Standardised response alternatives (True/False). 5 types: social traits, adjustment, pathological, screening, attitudes/interests/values.
Strengths: objectivity, easy administration, greater reliability, multi-dimensional.
Weaknesses: deliberate deception, social desirability (Marlowe-Crowne, 1964), response set.
Methods to overcome: Rapport, Forced-choice, Concealing purpose, Verification/correction keys. Acquiescence response set.
Firsts: Galton (1880); Woodworth (1918).

16 PF (Cattell, 1995)

Factor analysis → 16 source traits (trait dimensions/continuums). Scores plotted as personality profile. Counseling/employment use.

MMPI-2 (Hathaway & McKinley, 1943)

567 statements (True/False/Cannot Say). 10 clinical scales + 8 validity scales. From mild (shyness) to serious (schizophrenia, depression). Items repeated in different form to detect faking. Most common personality inventory.

MBTI (Assessment)

4 dimensions: S/I, T/F, I/E, P/J. 16 types (ISTJ, ISTP, ISFP, ISFJ, etc.). Career assessment. Commercially available.

NEO-PI-R (Costa & McCrae, 1992)

Five-Factor Model. 5 dimensions × 6 facets × 8 items = 240 items. OCEAN. Most widely used. NEO-FFI = 60-item truncated.

Projective Techniques

Frank (1939) coined "projective technique." Ambiguous stimuli; reveal unconscious. No correct/incorrect answer.
Frank's 5 categories: Constructive, Constitutive, Cathartic, Interpretative, Refractive.
Lindzay (1959): Association, Construction, Completion, Expressive, Choice/Ordering.
Strengths: indirect method; circumvent defenses.
Weaknesses: poorly standardised; low reliability; subjective interpretation; little validity evidence. Rorschach ranked 2nd, TAT 4th in usage.

Rorschach Inkblot (1921)

10 inkblots (5 black, 5 coloured). 3 stages: Performance Proper, Inquiry, Scoring. Location (W/D/Dd), Determinants (F/C/S/M), Content (H/A/Hd/Ad). Response productivity ratio (R): 15–30. Exner Comprehensive System (1969). Holtzman HIT (1961): 2 parallel forms, 45 cards, 22 variables.

TAT (Morgan & Murray, 1935)

20 B&W pictures. Client tells story (present, cause, past, future). "Hero" = identified character. Murray's needs (achievement, affiliation, aggression) + press (environmental forces). Construction technique.

Behavioural Assessment

Direct observation ("sine qua non"). Naturalistic observation (observer bias; Kazdin, 1982). Situational tests (OSS first; leadership, dominance). CEI (Hartshorne, May & Shuttleworth, 1930): altruism, honesty, self-control. Duplicating technique (cheating detection). Limitations: time-consuming, observer bias, "what to observe," visible vs invisible observer.
