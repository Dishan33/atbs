Concept & Definition of Personality
Allport (1937, 1961) · Kluckhohn & Murray (1953)
Marks Range TIER 1
"Dynamic organisation within the person of the psychophysical systems that determine unique adjustments to environment." Two pillars: Individuality + Consistency. Character = moral judgement; Temperament = inborn characteristics.
Key terms
  • dynamic organisationpsychophysical systemsindividualityconsistencycharacter vs temperamentAllport (1937)
Three Determinants of Personality
J.B. Watson
Marks Range TIER 1
Biological × Psychological × Environmental (multiplicative). Watson: extreme environmentalist. Biological factors set the boundary; psychological and environmental shape within it.
Key terms

Biological Determinants
Adler · Sheldon · Brislin & Lewis (1968)
Marks Range TIER 1
5 factors: Body build (somatotypes), Physical attractiveness, Homeostasis, Physical defects (Adler's organ inferiority), Health conditions. Anoxia → emotional outbursts. Obesity → social handicap.
Key terms

Nunnally's Four Types of Traits
Nunnally (1979)
Marks Range TIER 3
Social traits (friendliness, dominance), Motives (non-biological drives = personality dynamics), Personal conceptions (attitudes, values), Adjustment (harmony, conformance). All four are correlated.
Key terms

Nomothetic vs Idiographic Approaches
Allport (1934) · Cattell · Eysenck · Freud
Marks Range TIER 1
Nomothetic: general laws via quantitative methods (experiments, correlation, psychometric tests). Cattell, Eysenck. Idiographic: unique individual via qualitative methods (case studies, interviews, observation). Allport, Freud. Allport identified nomothetic approach to testing (1934) but promoted idiographic for understanding individuals. Modern researchers use both.
Key terms
  • nomotheticidiographicquantitative vs qualitativeAllport (1934)general lawscase studies
Neurophysiological Basis of Traits and Types
Eysenck · Corcoran (1964) · Aleksandrov & Shchukina (1992)
Marks Range TIER 3
E: ARAS (higher arousal in introverts). N: limbic system (lower thresholds in neurotics). P: gonadal hormones/MAO. Evidence: Lemon Drop Test (saliva 2× in introverts), EEG (higher alpha suppression), Aleksandrov & Shchukina — different character accentuations have varying curability.
Key terms
  • ARASlimbic systemcortical arousalYerkes-Dodson LawMAOLemon Drop Test

Four-Humour Theory
Hippocrates & Galen (~5th century BC – 190 AD)
Marks Range TIER 2
4 humours: Blood→Sanguine (cheerful, optimistic), Phlegm→Phlegmatic (apathetic, sluggish), Black bile→Melancholic (sad, brooding), Yellow bile→Choleric (irritable, excitable). Baseless today.
Key terms
  • HippocratesGalensanguine/phlegmatic/melancholic/cholericfour humours
Sheldon's Somatotype Theory
William Sheldon (1942)
Marks Range TIER 2
Ectomorph (thin→artistic, introverted; cerebrotonic), Endomorph (round→relaxed, sociable; viscerotonic), Mesomorph (muscular→dominant, energetic; somatotonic). 1–7 scale. Weak methodology (Tylor, 1965).
Key terms
  • Sheldon (1942)ectomorph/cerebrotonicendomorph/viscerotonicmesomorph/somatotonic
Jungian Types & MBTI
Carl Jung (1922) · Myers-Briggs · Keirsey
Marks Range TIER 2
4 dimensions: E/I, S/N, T/F, J/P. 16 personality types. Jung's task = Individuation. Keirsey temperaments: Guardian, Rational, Idealist, Artisan.
Key terms
  • Jung (1922)MBTIE/I, S/N, T/F, J/P16 typesindividuationKeirsey temperaments
Type A & Type B Personalities
Meyer Friedman
Marks Range TIER 2
Type A = workaholic, driven, impatient (coronary heart disease risk). Type B = laid back, easy going. Origin: Friedman noted impatient patients wore out front edges of waiting room sofas.
Key terms
  • Meyer FriedmanType AType Bcoronary heart disease
Introvert vs Extrovert — Eysenck’s Type Dimension
Eysenck · Corcoran (1964) · Wilson (1978)
Marks Range TIER 2
Introverts: higher baseline cortical arousal via ARAS, avoid stimulation, prefer scientific vocations, higher college grades. Extraverts: lower arousal, seek stimulation, prefer people-oriented jobs, greater pain tolerance. Yerkes-Dodson Law: inverted-U. Lemon Drop Test: introverts secrete ~2× more saliva.
Key terms
  • ARAScortical arousalYerkes-Dodson LawLemon Drop TestWilson (1978)
📊Trait Theories6 theories▾
Allport's Trait Theory
Gordon Allport (1937, 1961)
Marks Range TIER 1
Lexical approach (17,953 words, Allport & Odbert 1936). Idiographic. Cardinal (pervasive), Central (~5–10 per person), Secondary (situation-specific). Proprium = self. Functional autonomy of motives. Seven propriate functions.
Key terms
  • Allport (1937)lexical approachcardinal/central/secondaryPropriumfunctional autonomyidiographic
Allport's Seven Propriate Functions
Allport
Marks Range TIER 1
(1) Bodily self (2) Self-identity (3) Self-esteem (4) Self-extension (5) Self-image (6) Rational coper (7) Propriate striving. Two modes: Opportunistic (reactive, biological) vs Propriate (proactive, psychological).
Key terms
  • bodily selfself-identityself-esteemself-extensionself-imagerational coperpropriate striving
Cattell's Trait Theory
Raymond Cattell (1950)
Marks Range TIER 1
Surface vs Source traits (16 PF). Ergs (10 innate) + Sentiments (environmental). MAVA (1/3 heredity, 2/3 environment). Specification Equation: R=f(S,P). L/Q/OT-data. Syntality (group traits). Global traits: Introversion-Extraversion, Openness, Conscientiousness.
Key terms
  • Cattell (1950)surface vs source16 PFergsMAVAspecification equationsyntality
Eysenck's Trait-Type Theory (PEN Model)
Hans Eysenck (1916–1997)
Marks Range TIER 1
4-level hierarchy: Specific responses → Habits → Traits → Superfactors. PEN (orthogonal). E via ARAS (introverts higher arousal; Yerkes-Dodson Law). N via limbic system. P via gonadal hormones/MAO. EPQ + Lie Scale. Lemon Drop Test (Corcoran, 1964). High I + High N → anxiety; High E + High N → psychopathic.
Key terms
  • EysenckPEN4-level hierarchyARASYerkes-Dodsonlimbic systemEPQLemon Drop TestMAO

Based on Spranger's "Types of Men." Six value types: Theoretical, Economic, Aesthetic, Social, Political, Religious. 45 questions, 20 minutes. "Deep level traits."

🎭Psychodynamic Theories6 theories▾
Freud — Structure of Personality
Sigmund Freud (1890s–1930s)
Marks Range TIER 1
Topographical: Conscious (~10%), Preconscious (~10–15%), Unconscious (~75–80%). Iceberg metaphor. Structural: Id (pleasure principle), Ego (reality principle), Superego (ego-ideal + conscience). Tripartite. Psychic determinism.
Key terms
  • Freudconscious/preconscious/unconsciousicebergid/ego/superegopleasure principlereality principleego-idealconscience
Freud — Defense Mechanisms
Sigmund Freud
Marks Range TIER 1
Denial, Repression, Regression, Reaction formation, Projection, Rationalisation, Intellectualisation, Displacement, Sublimation (most adaptive — greatest civilisational achievements).
Key terms
  • denialrepressionregressionreaction formationprojectionrationalisationsublimation (most adaptive)
Freud — Psychosexual Development
Sigmund Freud
Marks Range TIER 1
5 stages: Oral (0–18mo), Anal (18mo–3yr), Phallic (3–6yr; Oedipus/Electra complexes, castration anxiety, identification), Latency (6yr–puberty), Genital (puberty+). Fixation = stuck; Regression = retreat under stress.
Key terms
  • oral/anal/phallic/latency/genitalerogenous zoneOedipus complexElectra complexcastration anxietyfixationregression
Karen Horney — Social Foundation
Karen Horney (1885–1952)
Marks Range TIER 2
Basic anxiety (isolated/helpless in hostile world). Basic evil → basic hostility. 10 neurotic needs. 3 coping strategies: Compliance (moving-toward), Aggression (moving-against), Withdrawal (moving-away-from). Real self vs Ideal self → "tyranny of the should." Womb envy. Neo-Freudian.
Key terms
  • Horneybasic anxietybasic evil/hostility10 neurotic needscompliance/aggression/withdrawaltyranny of the shouldwomb envyneo-Freudian
Sullivan — Interpersonal Theory
Harry Stack Sullivan (died 1949)
Marks Range TIER 3
Interpersonal model (vs Freud's drive model). 3 levels of cognition: Prototaxic, Parataxic, Syntaxic. Self-system (security operations: dissociation, selective inattention). Personifications: Good-me, Bad-me, Not-me. 7 developmental epochs. Euphoria = no anxiety. "Mothering one."
Key terms
  • Sullivaninterpersonalprototaxic/parataxic/syntaxicself-systemgood-me/bad-me/not-me7 epochsmothering oneeuphoria
Developmental Epochs (Sullivan)
Harry Stack Sullivan
Marks Range TIER 3
Seven epochs (timing by social environment, not biology): Infancy (0–1), Childhood (1–5), Juvenile (6–8), Pre-adolescence (9–12, chumship), Early adolescence (13–17), Late adolescence (18–22), Adulthood (23+). Self-system develops to protect against anxiety.
Key terms

⚙️Learning & Cognitive Theories4 theories▾
Pavlov — Classical Conditioning
Ivan Pavlov (1849–1936)
Marks Range TIER 2
UCS→UCR; CS paired with UCS→CR. Acquisition, Generalisation, Discrimination, Extinction, Spontaneous recovery, Higher-order conditioning. Experimental neurosis (Shenger-Krestovnika, 1921). Applications: systematic desensitisation, flooding, aversion therapy.
Key terms
  • PavlovUCS/UCR/CS/CRacquisitiongeneralisation/discriminationextinctionexperimental neurosissystematic desensitisation
Skinner — Operant Conditioning
B.F. Skinner (1904–1990)
Marks Range TIER 2
Operant vs Respondent behaviour. Positive/negative reinforcers. Schedules: continuous, fixed/variable ratio/interval (variable interval = most resistant). Shaping. Superstitious behaviour. Token economy. Chomsky (1959) critique: language not explained. Boulding (1984): animal principles may not apply to humans.
Key terms
  • Skinneroperant/respondentreinforcerschedulesvariable interval (most resistant)shapingtoken economyChomsky critique
Bandura — Social Cognitive Theory
Albert Bandura (b. 1925)
Marks Range TIER 3
Triadic reciprocal determinism (P↔B↔E). Self-efficacy (4 sources: mastery experiences, social modeling, social persuasion, psychological responses). Bobo doll experiment. Observational learning: 4 steps (attentional, retentional, reproduction, motivational). Vicarious learning. Symbolic modeling.
Key terms
  • Banduratriadic reciprocal determinismself-efficacyBobo dollobservational learningmodeling (4 steps)vicarious learning
Social-Cognitive Theory of Personality
Bandura · Mischel (1968)
Marks Range TIER 2
Triadic reciprocal determinism (P↔B↔E). Self-efficacy: belief in capabilities. Mischel’s 5 cognitive person variables: encoding strategies, expectancies, subjective values, self-regulatory systems, competencies. Goes beyond S-R behaviourism — includes cognition, self-regulation, vicarious learning. Initiated person-situation debate.
Key terms
  • triadic reciprocal determinismself-efficacyMischel’s variablesP-B-E interaction
🌟Humanistic Theories3 theories▾
Maslow — Hierarchy of Needs
Abraham Maslow (1908–1970)
Marks Range TIER 1
"Third Force." 5 levels: Physiological→Safety→Belongingness→Esteem (lower=respect of others, higher=self-respect)→Self-actualisation. D-needs (first 4; homeostasis). B-needs (self-actualisation; continuous). Self-actualisers: reality-centered, autonomous, unhostile humour, peak experiences, democratic value. Regress under stress. Metapathologies.
Key terms
  • Maslowhierarchy of needsD-needs vs B-needsself-actualisationThird Forcepeak experiencesmetapathologies
Rogers — Person-Centered / Self Theory
Carl Rogers (1902–)
Marks Range TIER 2
Self = organised perceptions of who I am. Real self vs Ideal self. Congruence = healthy; Incongruence = anxiety. Unconditional positive regard (vs conditional). Fully functioning person: open to experience, lives existentially, trusts organism. Three therapeutic conditions: genuineness, unconditional positive regard, empathic understanding.
Key terms
  • Rogersperson-centeredreal self vs ideal selfcongruenceunconditional positive regardfully functioning personclient-centered therapy
The Self — Real and Ideal Self (Rogers)
Carl Rogers
Marks Range TIER 3
Self = organised perceptions of who I am. Real self = who we are. Ideal self = who we want to be. Congruence (overlap) = healthy. Incongruence (gap) = anxiety, defensiveness. Measurement: Q-sort technique. Unconditional positive regard bridges the gap.
Key terms
  • real selfideal selfcongruenceincongruenceQ-sort

3 categories: Subjective, Objective, Projective. 4 technical criteria: Standardisation, Norms, Reliability (test-retest, split-half, parallel forms, inter-scorer; >+.70), Validity (content, predictive, concurrent, construct [convergent, divergent]).

Various
Marks Range TIER 1
Standardised response alternatives. Fake good vs Fake bad. Social desirability (Marlowe-Crowne, 1964). Response set. Methods to overcome: rapport, forced-choice, concealing purpose, verification keys. Filler items. Acquiescence response set.
Key terms

Rorschach Inkblot Test
Hermann Rorschach (1921) · Exner (1969)
Marks Range TIER 1
10 inkblots (5 black, 5 coloured). 3 stages: Performance Proper, Inquiry, Scoring. Location (W/D/Dd), Determinants (F/C/S/M), Content (H/A/Hd/Ad). Response productivity ratio (R): 15–30 for adults. Exner Comprehensive System (1969). Holtzman HIT (1961): 2 parallel forms, 45 cards, 22 variables.
Key terms
  • Rorschach (1921)10 inkblotsW/D/DdF/C/S/MExner (1969)Holtzman HIT
Thematic Apperception Test (TAT)
Morgan & Murray (1935)
Marks Range TIER 1
20 B&W pictures. Client tells story: what happening, what caused, past, future. "Hero" = character respondent identifies with. Content analysed via Murray's needs (achievement, affiliation, aggression) and press (environmental forces). Construction technique (Lindzay, 1959).
Key terms
  • Morgan & Murray (1935)TAT20 picturesheroneedspressLindzay (1959)
MMPI-2 — Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory
Hathaway & McKinley (1943) · Butcher et al. (2000)
Marks Range TIER 2
567 statements (True/False/Cannot Say). 10 clinical scales + 8 validity scales. Items repeated in different form to detect faking. Most common personality inventory. Tests from mild (shyness) to serious (schizophrenia, depression).
Key terms
  • MMPI-2Hathaway & McKinley (1943)567 statements10 clinical scales8 validity scales
Projective Techniques — Frank's Classification
Frank (1939) · Lindzay (1959)
Marks Range TIER 1
Frank coined "projective technique." 5 categories: Constructive, Constitutive, Cathartic, Interpretative, Refractive. Lindzay's 5: Association, Construction, Completion, Expressive, Choice/Ordering. Strengths: indirect, circumvent defenses. Weaknesses: poorly standardised, low reliability.
Key terms
  • Frank (1939)projective technique5 categoriesLindzay (1959)circumvent defenses
Behavioural & Situational Assessment
Hartshorne, May & Shuttleworth (1930)
Marks Range TIER 1
Direct observation, Naturalistic observation (observer bias; Kazdin 1982). Situational tests (OSS first; leadership, dominance). CEI: altruism, honesty, self-control. Duplicating technique for cheating detection. Limitations: time-consuming, observer bias, "what to observe," visible vs invisible observer.
Key terms
  • naturalistic observationsituational testsOSSCEIduplicating techniqueobserver bias
Reliability & Validity in Detail
Anastasi (1968) · Campbell & Fiske (1959) · Cronbach & Meehl (1955)
Marks Range TIER 2
Reliability: Test-retest (Anastasi 1968; >+.70), Split-half (internal consistency), Parallel forms, Inter-scorer (Yin 1984). Validity: Content, Predictive, Concurrent (criterion contamination), Construct (Cronbach & Meehl 1955). Convergent + Divergent/Discriminant (Campbell & Fiske 1959).
Key terms

Interview Method as a Personality Assessment Tool
White & Speisman (1982)
Marks Range TIER 2
Oldest and most widely used method. Face-to-face dialogue. Structured (prescribed order, preferred in research) vs Unstructured (flexible, preferred in therapy). Strengths: rapport, rich data, follow-up. Weaknesses: subjectivity, halo effect, interviewer bias. Halo effect = positive characteristics influence all assessments.
Key terms
  • structured/unstructured interviewhalo effectWhite & Speisman (1982)rapport
Case Study Method in Personality Assessment
Le Play (1806–1882) · Freud · Rogers
Marks Range TIER 3
Detailed study of single individual over extended period. Preserves unitary character. Types: Individual vs Community; Deviant case analysis vs Isolated clinical case analysis (e.g., Freud’s Little Hans). Strengths: rich, idiographic, rare phenomena. Weaknesses: limited generalisability, no cause-effect certainty, retrospective data distortion.
Key terms
  • case studyLe PlayFreud’s Little Hansdeviant case analysislimited generalisability
