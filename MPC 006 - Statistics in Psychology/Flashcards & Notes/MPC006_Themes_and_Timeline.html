MPC-006 · Statistics in Psychology

A methods atlas, not a formula list.

Start with the question, identify the kind of data and the design, then choose the statistic whose assumptions fit. The same route also gives an answer structure for theory and procedure questions.

The four routes through the course

01

Describe

Organise raw data with classification, frequency distributions, graphs, central tendency and dispersion.

Data → summary

Ask: What does this distribution look like?

02

Test a claim

State H₀ and H₁, set a significance level, select the sampling distribution and make a probability-based decision.

Hypothesis → decision

Ask: Is the observed result likely under H₀?

03

Relate & predict

Use correlation to describe covariation and regression to predict a criterion from one or more predictors.

Variables → association

Ask: How, and how strongly, do variables vary together?

04

Compare groups

Use t and ANOVA when their assumptions are met; use rank or frequency procedures when they are not.

Design → comparison

Ask: Which groups, conditions or frequencies are being compared?

Selection rules you should be able to explain

Two independent groups

Parametric route: independent-samples t test for interval/ratio scores with appropriate assumptions. Non-parametric route: Mann–Whitney U when scores are ranked or the assumptions are unsuitable.

Two related measurements

Parametric route: paired-samples t test for paired scores. Non-parametric route: Wilcoxon signed-rank test for paired ranks or non-normal paired differences.

Three or more groups

Parametric route: one-way ANOVA for one factor; two-way ANOVA when two factors and their interaction matter. Non-parametric route: Kruskal–Wallis for independent ranked groups.

Association

Pearson r for a linear association between continuous variables; Spearman rho or Kendall tau for ranks; phi for two dichotomies; point-biserial for one true dichotomy and one continuous variable.

Control and prediction

Partial correlation controls a specified third variable; part correlation removes a control from one variable. Multiple correlation/regression uses several predictors; regression gives a prediction equation.

Counts, not scores

Chi-square compares observed and expected frequencies. It is used with nominal-frequency data; the conclusion is about the departure of observed frequencies from expectation.

A defensible calculation-and-write-up sequence

Read the design

Identify variables, scale of measurement, number of groups and whether observations are independent or paired.

Name the test

State the statistic and give the selection reason: data type, design and assumptions.

State hypotheses

Write H₀ precisely; state H₁ as directional or non-directional where relevant.

Set α

Specify the level of significance and whether the decision is one-tailed or two-tailed.

Compute & compare

Show the essential terms, degrees of freedom where applicable, and the critical or obtained value.

Conclude in words

Reject or retain H₀, then translate the result back to the psychological variables without claiming causation from correlation.

Exam reminder: a high-scoring answer does not merely name a test. Define it, state its assumptions, outline its procedure, interpret the result, and note a suitable limitation or alternative.
