Key Strategic Insights

1. Begin with the data and design. Most test-choice questions become straightforward once you identify scale of measurement, number of groups, independence and whether the question concerns association or difference.
2. Correlation is a large family. Revise Pearson alongside rank, dichotomous, partial/part and multiple forms. The test is chosen by the variables, not by a remembered name.
3. ANOVA answers need variance logic. Explain why several means are compared through an F ratio, then state the assumptions and what a significant result does—and does not—show.
4. Non-parametric answers must name the alternative. State the relevant independent/related/three-group/frequency design before presenting Mann–Whitney, Wilcoxon, Kruskal–Wallis or chi-square.
