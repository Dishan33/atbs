Method atlas · from question to claim

Match the question to the method.

Your question sets the design. The design limits the claim. Quality checks show how much confidence you can place in that claim.

Use the decision map for exam comparisons. Follow the timeline to build a psychological study from a focused question. Each method answers a different kind of question.

DESCRIBE

“What is happening?” → survey, case study, observation.

ASSOCIATE

“What varies with what?” → correlational design.

EXPLAIN CAUSE

“Does X change Y?” → experiment; cautiously ex-post facto or quasi-experiment.

INTERPRET MEANING

“How is this lived or constructed?” → ethnography, grounded theory, discourse analysis.

The research sequence.

For a 10-mark answer on research process or research design, use this ordered structure. Each decision creates the conditions for the next one.

01
BLOCK 1

Locate a researchable problem

Move from a broad interest to a focused question. Read theory and prior findings so the problem matters, is feasible and avoids reinventing an answered question.

02
BLOCK 1

Build a conceptual frame

Define constructs, identify relevant variables and decide whether a hypothesis is appropriate. A hypothesis must be clear, testable and anchored in theory or evidence.

03
BLOCK 1

Operationalise and measure

Turn constructs into observable indicators. Ask whether the instrument is reliable (consistent) and valid (measures the intended construct).

04
BLOCK 1

Choose people and protect them

Specify population and sampling strategy. Address consent, confidentiality, risk, objectivity safeguards and the implications of selection for generalisation.

05
BLOCKS 2–3

Match the design to the claim

Use an experiment for a controlled causal claim; survey or correlation for description/association; ex-post facto or quasi-experiment when manipulation is unavailable; case study for depth.

06
BLOCK 3

Control variance deliberately

In experiments, maximise systematic variance, control extraneous variance and minimise error. Use randomisation, matching, counterbalancing or appropriate comparison groups.

07
BLOCK 4

Stay open to meaning

When studying culture, experience or language, choose qualitative methods. Record context, use reflexivity and show how interpretations arise from data rather than personal assumption.

08
BLOCKS 1 & 4

Analyse, interpret and report

Analyse in a way that fits the data. Separate finding from inference, state limitations, support claims with evidence and report procedures transparently.

Method pairs to keep separate.

These distinctions reappear as direct questions and as the evaluation layer in long answers.

CONTROL

Experiment vs ex-post facto

  • Experiment: manipulates IV; stronger causal inference.
  • Ex-post facto: examines existing differences; practical where manipulation is impossible or unethical.
  • Never claim equivalent control for both.
ASSOCIATION

Correlation vs causal comparison

  • Correlation: measures covariation between variables.
  • Causal-comparative: compares pre-existing groups on an outcome.
  • Neither alone removes alternative explanations.
REPEATED MEASURES

Between vs within subject

  • Between: different participants per condition; no carryover.
  • Within: same participants; controls individual differences.
  • Counterbalance within-subject order effects.
KNOWLEDGE

Quantitative vs qualitative

  • Quantitative: numerical measurement and statistical testing.
  • Qualitative: meanings, process, context and interpretive evidence.
  • Choose by question, not by a false hierarchy.
