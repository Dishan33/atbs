PYQ corpus · June 2011 to December 2025

Let the papers set the priority.

This page maps the confirmed 65-page MPC-005 corpus. Use it to see topics that repeat in long, medium and short answers. It does not predict the next paper.

Examiners ask you to define a method, set out its steps or types, distinguish it from a related design and use a small example. Prepare high-frequency topics for 10, 6 and 3 marks.

15

exam sessions from June 2011 to December 2025, covering changes in paper pattern.

4

durable priority blocks: foundations, field methods, designs and qualitative inquiry.

3

answer modes to rehearse: define, compare and explain a process or set of steps.

Topic cluster Priority Evidence from corpus What an answer must do
Research process & good research ESSENTIAL Long answers in 2011, 2013, 2015, 2017, 2019, 2023–25. Use the ordered research sequence; explain objectivity, bias safeguards and why each step follows the previous one.
Reliability & validity ESSENTIAL Reliability in 2012, 2013, 2015, 2017–18, 2021–25; validity/threats in most years. Define first, name methods or threats, then distinguish reliability from validity or internal from external validity.
Variables, constructs & hypothesis HIGH 2013, 2015, 2016, 2021–23, 2025; short notes recur too. Give a psychological example. Keep construct vs variable and extraneous vs confounded separate.
Sampling HIGH 2012, 2015, 2016, 2018, 2020, 2023 and 2025. State purpose, organise probability/non-probability methods, then show one limitation relevant to generalisation.
Survey research ESSENTIAL Asked in every broad phase of the corpus; steps, types, instruments and questions all recur. Use a numbered sequence. Distinguish cross-sectional and longitudinal forms, then mention instrument precautions.
Ex-post facto & field experiment ESSENTIAL Ex-post facto: 2012–25 repeatedly. Field experiment: 2011, 2013, 2015, 2018, 2020–25. Contrast manipulation/control with natural or pre-existing conditions; give strengths, limits and a brief example.
Case study HIGH 2014–18, 2023–24; appears as 10, 6 and 3 marks. Explain selection, sources of evidence and depth/context; avoid claiming automatic generalisability.
Single-factor design HIGH 2013–14, 2016–17, 2021–23 and 2025. Define factor and levels, then contrast between-group and within-subject designs and address counterbalancing.
Factorial design ESSENTIAL 2011–12, 2014–15, 2017–18, 2020–21, 2023–25. Explain factors/levels, show a 2 × 2 example, then distinguish main from interaction effects.
Quasi-experimental design ESSENTIAL 2011–13, 2015–16, 2018, 2021, 2024–25. Define lack of full random control, name types, then balance practical value against internal-validity limitations.
Correlational & comparative design HIGH 2012–18, 2020–21, 2023–24. Do not infer causation. Compare the design purpose, data structure and limit with experiment or causal comparison.
Ethnography ESSENTIAL 2012, 2015–17, 2019, 2022–25 across all mark bands. Definition → assumptions → fieldwork steps → ethics and reflexivity. Anchor it in culture/community.
Grounded theory & coding ESSENTIAL Every phase from 2011 to 2025, frequently as 10 marks. State theory generation, constant comparison, coding stages and theoretical sampling; link the stages coherently.
Discourse analysis ESSENTIAL 2011–12, 2015–18, 2021–25; often a full 10-mark answer. Explain language as social action; lay out approach/steps, context and relevance rather than treating it as mere content summary.
Qualitative data analysis & reporting ESSENTIAL 2011–16, 2018–21 and 2023–25. Separate data management, coding, interpretation and evaluation. Include credibility/reflexivity and report structure.

The three answer frames

10 marks: definition → organised conceptual explanation/steps → named distinction or method feature → short psychological example → limitation or conclusion.

6 marks: definition → four to six labelled points → one contrast, condition or application.

3 marks: exact definition → two key features or a sharp distinction. Do not spend the whole answer on a vague example.
