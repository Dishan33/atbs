Research foundations

One-page revision sheet

Research process

  1. Identify and delimit the problem.
  2. Review literature and frame concepts.
  3. Formulate hypothesis or research question.
  4. Operationalise variables and choose design.
  5. Sample, collect data, analyse and interpret.
  6. Conclude, report and publish.

Safeguards: standardisation, operational definitions, control, transparency and peer review reduce bias.

Reliability and validity

Reliability: consistency of measurement. Estimate it through test-retest, parallel forms, split-half/internal consistency and inter-rater agreement.

Validity: whether a measure or study supports the intended inference.

Internal validity threats: history, maturation, testing, instrumentation, regression, selection and attrition.

External validity: generalisation across people, settings and time.

Variables and hypothesis

Construct: abstract concept, such as anxiety. Variable: a measurable or manipulable feature.

  • IV: presumed cause/manipulated factor
  • DV: measured outcome
  • Extraneous variable: may affect DV
  • Confound: changes with IV and prevents separation

A hypothesis states a clear, testable prediction about variables.

Sampling

Population is the full group; a sample is the group studied.

Probability: simple random, systematic, stratified, cluster.

Non-probability: convenience, purposive, quota, snowball.

State how sampling affects generalisation and selection bias.

PYQ answer frame: Define → organise methods/types → give a short psychology example → state one limit or implication. High-priority repeats: research process, reliability, validity threats, variables and sampling.
