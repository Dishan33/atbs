MPC-005 · BLOCK 2

Types of research

One-page revision sheet

Survey research

Non-experimental, descriptive research using questionnaires or interviews.

  1. Set aims and define population.
  2. Design and pilot instrument.
  3. Select sample and administer survey.
  4. Code, analyse, interpret and report.

Types: cross-sectional; longitudinal trend, cohort and panel.

Use neutral, single-issue questions. Avoid leading and double-barrelled wording.

Ex-post facto

Studies possible causes after the fact, using pre-existing groups or conditions.

Steps: state problem; identify groups; select/match where possible; gather data; compare outcomes; interpret with caution.

Contrast: experiments manipulate the IV; ex-post facto studies do not.

Experimental and field research

An experiment manipulates an IV and measures a DV while controlling alternatives.

A field experiment manipulates a variable in a natural setting.

Field strength: natural behaviour. Limit: reduced control, practical and ethical constraints.

Case study

An intensive contextual study of an individual, group, event or institution.

Use interviews, observation, records and documents. Triangulate evidence.

Strength: depth and context. Limit: findings do not generalise automatically.

PYQ answer frame: Define the method → set out steps/types → contrast with a nearby method → add one strength and limit. Survey, ex-post facto and field experiment repeat often.
