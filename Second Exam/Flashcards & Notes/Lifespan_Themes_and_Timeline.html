🏗️Foundations of Lifespan Development2 topics▾
Issues, Areas & Approaches to Lifespan Development
Paul Baltes · Tennant & Pogson (1995) · Boyd & Bee (2006)
🎯 Likely 10 Marks
Three issues: Continuity vs. Discontinuity · Stability vs. Change · Nature vs. Nurture. Baltes' 11 characteristics: lifelong, multidimensional, multidirectional, plasticity, contextual, multidisciplinary, growth/maintenance/regulation, embedded in history, normative age-graded, normative history-graded, non-normative events. Four areas: Physical · Psychological/Cognitive · Social/Emotional · Sexuality/Gender. Four forces: Biological · Psychological · Socio-cultural · Life cycle.

Research Methods in Lifespan Development
Various
🎯 Likely 3 Marks
Four methods: Longitudinal (same individual over time) · Cross-sectional (different ages, same time — faster/cheaper) · Sequential (combines both — "best method") · Time-lag (different age groups, different years). Five obstacles: Representative sample · Establishing rapport · Appropriate methodology · Accuracy of data · Ethical aspects.
🤰Prenatal & Physical Development3 topics▾
Prenatal Development — Characteristics, Periods & Environmental Influences
Various · Knobloch & Pasamanick (1974) · Jensen et al. (1981)
🎯 Likely 10 Marks
Three periods: Zygote (fertilisation → 2 wks; 46 chromosomes) · Embryo (2 wks → 2 months; tissue differentiation; critical period for teratogens) · Fetus (2 months → birth; quickening at ~20 wks; viable at ~25 wks). Environmental influences: Mother's diseases (rubella) · Drugs/alcohol (FAS) · Radiation · Age (>35 risk) · Nutrition · Stress · Rh incompatibility.

Motor Development — Gross & Fine Motor Skills
Hurlock (1978)
🎯 Likely 6 Marks
Gross motor (large muscles: running, jumping) learnt before fine motor (small muscles: writing, drawing). Sensitive period: time when specific activity learnt most effectively. Not force writing before 4.5–5.5 yrs. Six basic behaviours: running, jumping, sequencing, balancing, throwing, catching. Laws: Cephalocaudal, Proximodistal.
Factors Influencing Height and Weight in Early Childhood
Hurlock (1978)
🎯 Likely 3 Marks
Seven factors: Family influences · Nutrition · Emotional disturbances (adrenal steroids inhibit growth hormone) · Socio-economic status · Health · Endocrine functioning (dwarfism/giantism) · Sex. Body types: endomorphic, mesomorphic, ectomorphic. Growth: 2–2.5 in + 3–6 lbs/year.
🧠Cognitive Development6 topics▾
Piaget's Theory of Cognitive Development
Jean Piaget (1896–1980)
🎯 Likely 10 Marks
4 stages: Sensorimotor (0–2; object permanence) → Pre-operational (2–7; egocentrism, symbols) → Concrete operational (7–11; conservation, decentration, reversibility) → Formal operational (11+; abstract, hypothetical; only 35% achieve). Key concepts: schemas → structures; assimilation vs. accommodation; egocentrism; horizontal décalage (Inhelder & Piaget, 1955/1958). Educational: discovery learning, manipulatives.

Sensori-Motor Stage in Cognitive Development
Piaget
🎯 Likely 3 Marks
Six sub-stages: Reflexes (0–1 mo) → Primary Circular Reactions (1–4 mo) → Secondary Circular Reactions (4–8 mo) → Coordination (8–12 mo; goal-directed) → Tertiary Circular Reactions (12–24 mo; trial-and-error) → Early Representational Thought (symbols). Object permanence at ~7 months.
Information Processing Approach in Cognitive Development
Herbert Simon · 1950s
🎯 Likely 10 Marks
Model: Sensory register → Working memory (7 items) → Long-term memory. Four beliefs: Thinking = encoding/storing/retrieving; Four mechanisms (encoding, strategy construction, automatisation, generalisation); Self-modification; Task analysis. Components: Attention · Perception (Embedded Figures Test, Witkin 1971) · Memory (rehearsal, elaboration, metamemory).
Language Development
Various
🎯 Likely 6 Marks
Four components: Phonology · Semantics (fast mapping, vocabulary spurt 18–24 mo, under/over-extensions) · Grammar (syntax + morphology; telegraphic speech) · Pragmatics (turn-taking, audience awareness). Comprehension before production (5-month lag).
Learning to Write as a Basic School Skill
Various · Bairstow (1989)
🎯 Likely 3 Marks
Handwriting improves 6–11 yrs. Eye-hand coordination: by age 8, better planning; by age 10, control of speed/direction (Bairstow, 1989). Not force before 4.5–5.5 yrs. Girls greater dexterity.
Grammar and Semantics
Burke et al. (1989)
🎯 Likely 3 Marks
Ages 6–12: vocabulary expansion, language structure refinement. 6–7-yr-olds confused by complex constructions. Language difficulties → aggressive behaviour (Burke et al., 1989). Communication effectiveness develops with cognitive/overall development.
⚖️Moral Development1 topic▾
Kohlberg's Theory of Moral Development
Lawrence Kohlberg · Piaget
🎯 Likely 10 Marks
Moral dilemmas method. Heinz dilemma. Analysed reasons, not answers. Three levels / six stages: Preconventional (Punishment-obedience; Instrumental-exchange) → Conventional (Good-boy-nice-girl; System-maintaining/law & order) → Postconventional (Social-contract; Universal ethical principles). Piaget: Heteronomous morality (6–7; imminent justice, objective consequences, absolutism) → Autonomous morality (~10; cooperation, motives considered). Hartshorne & May (1928–30): moral standards ≠ moral behaviour.

❤️Social-Emotional Development3 topics▾
Attachment Patterns
Ainsworth et al. (1978)
🎯 Likely 3 Marks
Attachment = emotionally toned relationship. Internal working model predicts adult romantic relationships. Three patterns: Secure (likable, open, trusting) · Avoidant (independent, mistrusting, emotional distance) · Resistant/anxious-ambivalent (fears of abandonment, smothering). Healthy relationships can heal troubled backgrounds.
Common Emotional Patterns in Childhood
Hurlock (1978) · Devadas & Jaya (1984)
🎯 Likely 3 Marks
Fear (shift from typical to general; shyness) · Anxiety (develops later; increases 4th–6th grade) · Anger (more frequent than fear; impulsive vs. inhibited responses) · Curiosity (instinctive foundation; "questioning age") · Joy (success most pleasant). Emotional tolerance = accept and adjust to unpleasant experiences. Patterns affected by health, intellect, environment, social reactions.
Social Development — Role of Parents, Siblings & Peers
Baumrind (1971)
🎯 Likely 10 Marks
Parents: role → consultants. Mothers preferred; fathers encourage independence. Household responsibilities → mature behaviour (Baumrind, 1971). Siblings: teach/help each other. Peer group: 6–9 yrs (small groups, gender segregation) → 9–12 yrs (crowds, cliques). Conformity peak at grades 8–9. Popularity: sociable, cooperative, assertive. Rejected: aggressive or withdrawn. Neglected: neither liked nor disliked.
🪪Identity & Adolescence3 topics▾
Identity Crisis & Marcia's Identity Statuses
Erik Erikson · James Marcia (1966, 1976, 1980)
🎯 Likely 10 Marks
Erikson: Identity vs. Role Confusion. Identity crisis = intensive analysis and exploration. Marcia (1966): Four statuses defined by Crisis × Commitment: Foreclosure (no crisis, commitment made) · Diffusion (±crisis, no commitment) · Moratorium (crisis, no commitment yet) · Achievement (crisis experienced, commitment made). NOT stages — not sequential. Lifespan construct: basis for adult expectations.

Adolescence — Challenges, Identity & Cognitive Development
Elkind (1967)
🎯 Likely 10 Marks
Three stages: Early (12–14; contradictory; search for identity) · Middle (14–17; friends pivotal; "Who am I?") · Late (17–19; firm identity; idealistic views). Challenges: independent identity; challenge parents; peer group belonging. Cognitive: formal operations (abstract, hypothetical, multidimensional, relative). Adolescent egocentrism (Elkind, 1967): imaginary audience.
Sexuality During Adolescence
Various
🎯 Likely 6 Marks
Puberty = biologically/sexually mature. Pituitary gland → FSH → testosterone (males) / estrogen (females). Males: testicle enlargement → penis → pubic hair → nocturnal emissions (~14 yr). Females: breast development → pubic hair → menarche (10–16.5 yr). Concerns: pregnancy, STDs, inappropriate behaviours. Need individualized sex education.
Self-Esteem & Self-Concept During Adolescence
Elkind (1967)
🎯 Likely 6 Marks
Self-concept = knowledge about self (becomes abstract, complex, organised into self-schemas). Three categories: Personal · Social · Self-ideals. Self-esteem ≠ self-concept: esteem = overall evaluation; concept = domain-specific perceptions. Self-concept is consequence, not cause, of high achievement. Imaginary audience (Elkind, 1967). Causes of low self-esteem: hereditary, living conditions, education, physiology, society, fear, disease.
👤Adulthood & Ageing5 topics▾
Middle Adulthood — Psychosocial Issues, Developmental Tasks & Cognitive Changes
Erikson · Levinson · Horn & Hofer (1992) · Sternberg
🎯 Likely 10 Marks
Age 40–60. Erikson: Generativity vs. Stagnation. Levinson: Transition (40–45) → Life structure → Re-evaluation → Culminating. Four tasks: Young-Old · Destruction-Creation · Masculinity-Femininity · Engagement-Separateness. Intelligence: Crystallized (verbal, vocabulary — improves) vs. Fluid (flexible reasoning, speed — declines). Marital satisfaction: U-curve. Possible selves. Androgyny.
Cognitive Changes During Adulthood (Early & Middle)
Schaie · Perry · Labouvie-Vief · Sternberg · Horn & Hofer (1992)
🎯 Likely 10 Marks
Early: Schaie (Achieving + Responsibility stages). Perry (dualistic → multiple → relative subordinate → full relativism). Post-formal thought. Labouvie-Vief (logic replaced with commitment). Middle: Multidirectional development. Crystallized vs. fluid. Sternberg's triarchic (analytic/creative/practical). Memory/processing decline. Religion/meaning (Victor Frankl). Cohort effects.
Psychosocial Changes — Early Adulthood & Old Age
Erikson · Levinson · Sternberg · Peck · Labouvie-Vief
🎯 Likely 6 Marks
Early adulthood: Intimacy vs. Isolation (Erikson). Levinson: life structure, dream, age-30 transition, social clock. Sternberg's Triangular Theory (intimacy + passion + commitment; passionate → companionate love). Old age: Ego Integrity vs. Despair. Peck's 3 tasks (ego differentiation, body transcendence, ego transcendence). Labouvie-Vief: emotional expertise. Reminiscence vs. life review. Social convoy.
Family Life Approach During Adulthood
Various
🎯 Likely 6 Marks
Launching children (no longer negative). U-curve of marital satisfaction. Feminisation of poverty. Grandparenthood meanings (valued elder, immortality, reinvolvement, indulgence). Siblings: declining contact; sister relationships closer. Friends: selective; quality over quantity. Gender identity shifts → androgyny.
Late Adulthood — Ageing Issues, Retirement & Leisure
Horn & Hofer (1992) · Schaie & Willis
🎯 Likely 6 Marks
Age 65+. Primary vs. secondary ageing. Physical: hair, skin, presbyopia, hearing loss. Cognitive: Selective optimisation with compensation. Terminal decline. Memory: slower processing; implicit > deliberate; autobiographical best recall of adolescence/early adulthood; prospective memory declines. Wisdom: practical knowledge, emotional maturity, increases with age. Remedial training reverses decline in 40–60% (Schaie & Willis).
⭐Special Topics4 topics▾
Learning Disability — Characteristics, Identification & Remedial Measures
Various
🎯 Likely 6 Marks
Disorder in basic psychological processes (language). Average to superior intelligence. Discrepancy between ability and achievement. Types: dyslexia, dysgraphia, dyscalculia. Identification: standardised tests, IQ-achievement discrepancy, observation. Remedial: IEP, multisensory instruction, assistive technology, peer tutoring.
Intellectual Disability (Mental Retardation) — Identification & Remedial Measures
Various
🎯 Likely 6 Marks
Subaverage intellectual functioning + deficits in adaptive behaviour + manifested before age 18. Levels: Mild (50–70), Moderate (35–50), Severe (20–35), Profound (<20). Causes: genetic (Down syndrome), prenatal (rubella), perinatal, postnatal. Identification: IQ tests (<70), adaptive behaviour scales. Remedial: early intervention, life skills training, vocational training, inclusive education.
Exceptional Children
Various
🎯 Likely 3 Marks
Children who deviate from normal and require specialised services. Categories: gifted/talented, learning disabilities, intellectual disability, physical disabilities, emotional/behavioural disorders, communication disorders, autism spectrum. Approaches: inclusive education, IEP, special schools, resource rooms, remedial teaching, assistive technology.
Value Education & Educational Excursions for School Children
Various
🎯 Likely 6 Marks
Value education: develop moral reasoning, empathy, character, social responsibility. Methods: storytelling, role-playing, group discussions, community service, value clarification. Educational excursions: experiential learning, real-world connections, social development, motivation. Language teaching: start young, free from fatigue, through games/puzzles/plays.
Early Childhood — Characteristics & Hazards
UNESCO
🎯 Likely 6 Marks
Early childhood = 2–6 years. Also: toy age, play age, school going age. UNESCO: birth to 8 years. Characteristics: obstinacy, stubbornness, negativistic, self-sufficient, independent. Physical hazards: illness, accidents, obesity. Psychological hazards: speech, social, play, moral hazards.
