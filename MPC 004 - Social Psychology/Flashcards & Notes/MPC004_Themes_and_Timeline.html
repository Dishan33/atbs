Advanced Social Psychology

See the threads, not just the topics.

This atlas links the course’s central questions—how people perceive, influence, help, harm, categorise and organise one another—to the thinkers and answer frames that recur in the PYQs.

The course in four questions

Block 1

How do we make sense of people?

Social cognition, person perception, schemas, attribution and research methods explain how social information becomes judgement.

  • Key contrast: person vs situation
  • Exam anchors: attribution error, cognitive algebra, ethics
Block 2

How do people alter one another?

Influence, attraction, helping, aggression and conflict show how interaction can coordinate, persuade, support or harm.

  • Key contrast: conformity vs compliance vs obedience
  • Exam anchors: Asch, Cialdini, empathy-altruism
Block 3

How do evaluations become social patterns?

Attitudes, stereotypes, prejudice and conflict connect individual evaluation to intergroup outcomes.

  • Key contrast: stereotype vs prejudice vs discrimination
  • Exam anchors: Heider, Katz, Bem, prejudice reduction
Block 4

How do groups organise social life?

Groups, dynamics, identity, interaction and collective behaviour show how shared membership changes action.

  • Key contrast: group vs crowd
  • Exam anchors: Tuckman, Tajfel & Turner, crowd theories

Timeline of high-yield ideas

Use this as a recall spine. In an answer, a named theorist or study should support the explanation—not replace it.

1908

Social psychology becomes a named discipline

Early textbooks associated with McDougall and Ross signal the emergence of social psychology as a distinct field between individual psychology and sociology.

1943

Lewin: behaviour in a field

Lewin’s field perspective foregrounds the interaction of person and environment, a durable idea for explaining social behaviour.

1950

Group interaction and status

Bales’s work on group interaction informs later expectation-states accounts of why some members become more influential than others.

1951

Asch and conformity

Unanimous majority pressure can shift public judgement even on an unambiguous task; the dissenter effect becomes central.

1958

Heider’s balance and attribution traditions

Consistency in interpersonal evaluations and causal explanations of behaviour become key routes into attitudes and person perception.

1960s

Obedience, helping and social learning

Studies and theories of authority, bystander intervention and modelling shift attention to powerful situational and learned influences.

1967

Bem’s self-perception theory

People may infer weak or unclear attitudes from observing their own behaviour and its context.

1969

Tuckman’s group development sequence

Forming, storming, norming, performing and adjourning become a practical structure for group-process answers.

1970s–80s

Identity, intergroup relations and helping

Social identity theory explains group-based self-concept and in-group bias; empathy-altruism research sharpens the study of prosocial motives.

Now

Social psychology as applied evidence

The course connects social mechanisms to prejudice reduction, conflict resolution, health, legal systems, organisations and community life.

Answer connections that earn marks

When asked about attitude change

Use function + consistency + inference

Link Katz (what the attitude does), Heider (pressure toward balance) and Bem (behaviour informing attitude). Then add source/message/recipient factors.

When asked about prejudice

Move from cognition to behaviour

Define stereotype, prejudice and discrimination separately; explain maintenance; then give contact, cooperation, recategorisation and norm change as reduction routes.

When asked about influence

Identify the route of pressure

Conformity = group norms; compliance = direct request; obedience = authority. Use Asch, Cialdini or obedience studies only where they fit.

When asked about group conflict

Explain both identity and structure

Include categorisation/identity as well as goals, resources, communication and power. Finish with a resolution mechanism.

PYQ rule of thumb:

Repeated questions are usually testing a durable distinction, named theory, classic study or practical application. Build your revision around those anchors—not only around chapter titles.
